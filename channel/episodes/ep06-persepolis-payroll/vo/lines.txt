Want to see the oldest payslip in Iran? It's buried under Persepolis.
About two and a half thousand years ago, King Darius started building Persepolis.
In nineteen thirty-three, archaeologists found stone boxes under the Apadana palace.
Inside? A gold tablet... and a silver one.
Darius wrote how far his empire stretched. Basically, an imperial résumé.
Under the boxes, there were coins too. Luxury flooring, apparently.
But the real treasure... is these clay tablets.
Thousands of them, full of accounts for the workers of Persepolis.
Who got barley, who got wine, who got sheep. Bookkeeping, in cuneiform!
So many of these workers were paid in rations and wages.
Even better? Women worked here too, and got their rations.
New mothers got extra rations. Maternity pay, Persian Empire edition!
Two and a half thousand years ago, this place had a payroll.
We lose payslips on our phones. They wrote theirs in clay, and it's still here!
