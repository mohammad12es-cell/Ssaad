This little gold coin once sent a Spartan king home. Let me explain.
Around five hundred B.C., Darius the Great had a problem.
His empire was huge, and everyone paid with different lumps of metal.
So he launched his own gold coin: the daric.
On it? The king himself, kneeling, with a bow. Very on brand.
The Greeks had a nickname for these coins: archers.
The gold was remarkably pure, so merchants trusted it everywhere.
Then the Spartan king Agesilaus marched into Persian lands in Asia Minor.
Persia didn't send more soldiers. It sent gold, to Sparta's rivals in Greece.
War broke out back home, and Agesilaus was called back.
According to Plutarch, he joked that he'd been driven out by thousands of archers.
Not soldiers. Coins!
Economics, lesson one: sometimes money is the strongest army.
Today, darics are treasured in museums around the world.
