In eighteen ninety-one, Iranians beat a monopoly by quitting the hookah.
The king, Naser al-Din Shah, needed money.
So he gave a British company control of Iran's entire tobacco trade, for fifty years.
Growing, buying, selling, exporting. All of it, one company.
Iranian merchants were furious. So were the farmers.
Protests spread from city to city.
Then a leading cleric reportedly declared that using tobacco was forbidden.
And the country actually stopped smoking!
Shops closed. Hookahs were put away. Reportedly, even inside the royal palace.
Imagine a nationwide hookah strike.
By early eighteen ninety-two, the Shah cancelled the deal.
The catch? Iran had to pay heavy compensation, and took out a foreign loan.
So the boycott won, but the bill still came.
Lesson: never underestimate customers who walk away.
