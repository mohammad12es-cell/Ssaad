One street in Tehran was named after a light bulb. Blame a merchant.
Over a hundred and twenty years ago, Tehran's nights meant lanterns, candles, and darkness.
Then came Haj Mohammad Hossein Amin al-Zarb, son of one of Iran's richest merchants.
Other rich men bought gardens and land. He bought a power generator.
Yes, really. He brought a generator all the way from abroad.
Around nineteen oh four, the lights came on.
People were amazed. A lamp with no oil, and no wick!
A few shops, some houses, and part of the street glowed at night.
And Tehranis, being very creative, named it Cheragh Bargh. Electric Light Street.
Very poetic. Very literal.
Later, the official name changed to Amir Kabir Street.
But many old Tehranis still say: I'm going to Cheragh Bargh.
One merchant's money changed a whole city's nights.
Next time you flip a light switch, thank Amin al-Zarb.
