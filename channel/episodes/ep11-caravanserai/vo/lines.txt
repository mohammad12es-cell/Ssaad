Before hotel apps, Persia had roadside hotels every day's ride. Here's the story.
For centuries, merchants crossed Iran in long camel caravans.
Deserts, mountains, bandits. Not exactly a relaxing road trip.
So rulers built caravanserais: fortified inns along the trade routes.
Picture this: one big gate, high walls, and a courtyard inside.
Camels in the courtyard, merchants in the rooms. Very organised.
Water, food, safety, and a place to trade before moving on.
In the sixteen hundreds, Shah Abbas the First built a huge network of them.
Legend says he built nine hundred and ninety-nine. Historians say: not quite.
Still, many were spaced about one day's journey apart.
So every evening, the caravan could reach the next stop.
Basically, a hotel chain, with free camel parking.
In twenty twenty-three, fifty-four Persian caravanserais became a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
Some of them are open again today, as real hotels!
