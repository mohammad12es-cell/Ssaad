In twelve ninety-four, Iran tried paper money, and it lasted about two months.
The ruler was Gaykhatu, a Mongol khan who ruled Iran.
His treasury was empty. Like, completely empty.
Then someone had an idea: China uses paper money. Why don't we?
So in Tabriz, they printed notes copied from Chinese money, called chao.
The rule? Everyone must use the paper. Gold and silver were banned.
Refuse the notes, and you could face severe punishment.
The result? Merchants simply closed their shops.
The great bazaar of Tabriz went silent. Caravans stopped coming.
People couldn't buy food with money they didn't trust.
Within weeks, the plan collapsed, and the notes were withdrawn.
Turns out, paper money only works if people believe in it.
Money isn't really paper or metal. It's trust.
Iran wouldn't try banknotes again for almost six hundred years.
