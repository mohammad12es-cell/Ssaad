One king turned silk into Iran's biggest export. Here's how.
In the sixteen hundreds, Persian silk was in demand from Europe to India.
Shah Abbas the First wanted that silk money flowing into Isfahan.
So he made the silk trade a royal monopoly.
But he needed expert traders, with connections everywhere.
In sixteen oh four, he forcibly moved thousands of Armenians from the town of Julfa.
It was a painful relocation. Many people suffered on the way.
In Isfahan, they built a new neighbourhood: New Julfa.
And these Armenian merchants built a trade network across the world.
From Amsterdam, to India, and even to Manila.
They sent letters, contracts, and caravans across continents.
Some historians call it one of the great trade networks of its time.
New Julfa's churches still stand in Isfahan today.
A neighbourhood born from hardship became a global business hub.
