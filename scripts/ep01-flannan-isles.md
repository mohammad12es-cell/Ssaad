# EP01 · The Lighthouse Where Three Men Vanished: and the Clues That Were Invented

**Channel:** The Last Log · **Target length:** ~12 min (≈1,750 words at 145 wpm) · **Status:** draft v1, needs a fact check

## Video idea
In December 1900, three keepers vanished from a brand-new lighthouse on a remote Scottish island. The story everyone retells (an overturned chair, an uneaten meal, a log full of terror) is partly **invented**. We tell the legend, then open the record, then decide what most likely happened.

## Title options
1. Three Men Vanished From This Lighthouse. The Famous Clues Were Fake.
2. The Flannan Isles Mystery: What the Records Actually Say
3. They Found the Lighthouse Empty. Then Someone Rewrote the Story.

**Pick #1.** It has the strongest curiosity gap and promises a twist, not just a retelling.

### ACT 1: THE ROCK (0:45–3:00)
**[S5]** VO: Twenty miles west of the Isle of Lewis, in the Outer Hebrides, there's a cluster of islands called the Flannans. The largest is Eilean Mòr: sheer cliffs, almost no flat ground, and waves that can climb more than a hundred feet up the rock in a storm. [CHECK: distance, wave height]

**[S6]** VO: In December 1899, a new lighthouse was lit on its summit. [CHECK: lit 7 Dec 1899] Three keepers worked it at a time, in rotation, with a fourth on leave ashore.

**[S7]** VO: In December 1900, the three on the rock were James Ducat, the principal keeper; Thomas Marshall; and Donald MacArthur, an occasional keeper filling in. [V]

**[S8]** VO: Their supplies, and the relief keeper, arrived by boat: when the sea allowed it. On Eilean Mòr, the sea rarely allowed it.

**[S9]** VO: Keep one detail in mind. The island had two landings, east and west, because no single landing was safe in every wind. Remember the west landing. It matters later.

### ACT 2: THE DARK LIGHT (3:00–5:30)
**[S10]** VO: On the night of the 15th of December 1900, the steamer *Archtor* passed the Flannans on its way to Leith. Her crew noticed something wrong. The light was out. [V]

**[S11]** VO: A lighthouse that doesn't shine isn't just a broken lamp. It's a danger to every ship on that coast. But the *Archtor* reached port days later, and the report moved slowly. [V: docked Leith ~3 days later]

**[S12]** VO: The relief boat, the *Hesperus*, was due around the 20th. Storms kept her in harbour. She finally reached Eilean Mòr on the 26th of December. [CHECK: dates]

**[S13]** VO: Her captain, James Harvey, sounded the horn and fired a rocket. Normally the keepers would come down to meet the boat. Nobody came. [V: Harvey; CHECK: horn/rocket]

**[S14]** VO: The relief keeper, Joseph Moore, was rowed ashore alone. He climbed the steps to the station. [V]

**[S15]** VO: The gate was closed. The door was closed. Inside, the fire had been out for some time. The beds were empty. [CHECK each detail against Moore's statement]

### ACT 3: HOW A LEGEND GETS BUILT (5:30–8:00)
**[S18]** VO: Now, here's where the story you've probably heard starts to drift away from the record.

**[S19]** VO: Popular versions describe a table set for a meal nobody finished, and a chair knocked over as if someone ran out in panic. [CHECK] Those images seem to come from a poem, *Flannan Isle*, by Wilfrid Wilson Gibson, published years later. [CHECK: 1912] It's a powerful poem. It is not a report.

**[S20]** VO: Then there's the logbook. Versions online quote dramatic entries: severe winds "never seen in twenty years," one keeper crying, another praying, and finally: "Storm ended, sea calm. God is over all."

**[S21]** VO: But the official log's last entry is dated the 13th of December. Weather notes up to 9 a.m. on the 15th were chalked on a slate, ready to copy into the book. [V] Those words are not among those official entries. They surfaced decades later, and the Northern Lighthouse Board has no record of them. [V: sources say no original copies held]

**[S22]** VO: And notice something strange: those fake entries describe a huge storm on the 12th and 13th. Yet the passing ships reported no unusual storm that night in that area. [CHECK] The legend didn't just add details. It added a storm.

**[S23]** VO: So if we strip away the poem and the forged log, what's left? An empty station. A stopped routine. One coat on a hook. And the west landing.

### ACT 4: THE WEST LANDING (8:00–10:30)
**[S24]** VO: On the 29th of December, Superintendent Robert Muirhead arrived to investigate. He had recruited these men himself. He knew them. [V]

**[S25]** VO: He went straight to the west landing. And there he found damage: at a height well above the normal sea. Iron railings bent. A crane damaged. A box that held ropes and equipment, set in a crevice high on the cliff, had been torn loose, and its ropes scattered over the rocks. [V: broken crane, dislodged equipment; CHECK: exact height and items]

**[S26]** VO: Muirhead's conclusion, in his report of the 8th of January 1901, was simple. The men had gone down to the west landing to secure that equipment during bad weather, and they were carried away by an unexpectedly large sea. [V]

**[S27]** VO: And the single coat? It fits. Ducat and Marshall went down in oilskins. MacArthur, perhaps seeing from above that something was going wrong, ran down to help without stopping for his. [Hypothesis: label as Muirhead's/our interpretation]

**[S28]** VO: That would also explain why the station was so orderly. No struggle, no panic. Just three men doing their job, in a place where the sea can rise without warning.

### ACT 5: WHAT WE STILL DON'T KNOW (10:30–12:00)
**[S29]** VO: Case closed? Not entirely. A rule said one keeper should always stay in the lighthouse. Why did all three leave? [CHECK: rule] And why was the weather on the slate calm enough that no one expected trouble? [CHECK]

**[S30]** VO: Some researchers suggest a rogue wave. Others, a man swept in and two lost trying to save him. We'll never know which. Their bodies were never found. [V]

**[S31]** VO: But here's what we do know. The truth of the Flannan Isles isn't a ghost, a sea serpent, or a page of hysterical last words. It's three ordinary men, on an extraordinary rock, who went out to protect the light: and didn't come back.

**[S32]** VO: The legend gave them a mystery. The record gives them something better: the dignity of what really happened.

**[S33]** VO: The light on Eilean Mòr still shines today. Automated since 1971. No keepers on the rock anymore. [CHECK: 1971]
